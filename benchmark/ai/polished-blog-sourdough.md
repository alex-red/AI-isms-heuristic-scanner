# Why my sourdough starter died twice

I killed my first starter in nine days. I fed it flour and tap water every morning, kept it on the counter next to the stove, and watched it bubble for a week before it went flat and smelled like nail polish remover.

The kitchen was the problem. Next to the stove it swung between 17°C overnight and 29°C after dinner, and the yeast could not keep up. Moving the jar to a shelf in the hallway, where the temperature stayed near 22°C, fixed it. The second starter died because I switched to a bleached flour I had on hand. The first one had been fed whole rye, which carries far more wild yeast and bacteria on its surface.

What worked in the end was boring. I weighed 50 grams of starter, 50 of water and 50 of rye every 24 hours, and wrote the time on a strip of masking tape on the jar. By day six it doubled in four hours after a feed. That is the signal I wait for before baking, not the calendar.

If your starter stalls, check three things before you throw it out: the room temperature, whether the flour is whole grain, and whether your tap water is heavily chlorinated. A day on a bottle of filtered water was enough to restart mine once.
